The House of IDARA

A name inherited.
A standard upheld.

In the Efik language of Calabar — a culture whose hospitality was once the measure of a household’s honour — idara names the thing held dearest: something precious. We took the word as a promise. Every commission that leaves our atelier must be worthy of it.

Founded in Lagos and serving clients across Nigeria and abroad, IDARA is a private gifting house: part curator, part confidant. We source from master artisans and storied maisons, compose each gift by hand, and deliver it with ceremony — so that what you give says what you mean.

“Our mission is simple: that no gesture entrusted to us should ever feel ordinary — and no recipient should ever forget how it felt to receive it.”

Questions, Answered

Before you ask

How far in advance should I commission a gift?

Bespoke commissions are best begun 2–3 weeks ahead; corporate programmes, 4–8 weeks. That said, our concierge desk regularly performs quiet miracles on 48 hours’ notice — ask.

Do you deliver outside Lagos and Abuja?

Yes. We deliver white-glove across Nigeria’s major cities and ship internationally via insured courier partners, with the same presentation standards wherever the gift lands.

What is the minimum investment?

Celebration Collection commissions begin at ₦250,000. Fully bespoke work is quoted per brief. We are candid about what a budget can achieve beautifully — and we never pad.

Can you keep the sender — or the surprise — confidential?

Discretion is a house rule, not a request. Anonymous gifting, sealed notes, unmarked delivery and strict confidentiality around occasions and budgets are all standard.

Do you handle corporate branding and compliance?

Yes — discreet co-branding, per-recipient value caps for regulated industries, itemised documentation for procurement, and gifting registers where policy requires them.

What if the recipient’s tastes are a mystery?

That is precisely our craft. A short consultation — who they are, how they live, what they never buy themselves — gives us enough. Learning the recipient is the service.
